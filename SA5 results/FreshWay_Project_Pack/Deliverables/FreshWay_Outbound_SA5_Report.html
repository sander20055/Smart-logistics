Management summary

The agreed direction is a company-wide AI platform. Outbound will use separately governed models for arrival risk and unloading duration. Its proposed trip agents will coordinate tentative planning options inside approved limits. The Outbound planner retains approval of every operational departure, route or stop-order change.

The historical data supports investigating ZE delivery reliability, but neither earlier scheduling nor AI has been shown to improve FreshWay performance. The next decision is to validate interfaces and demonstrate bounded coordination in shadow mode. Procurement and live operation follow only after requirements, costs, access, safety and evaluation criteria are agreed.

Interpretation and limits

ZE deliveries have less remaining scheduling margin. This association supports testing feasible earlier planning, but does not establish the cause of each late arrival. All appointments have a constant +15-minute planned offset; its operational reason remains unverified. Thirty- and sixty-minute appointments must also be distinguished from broader store acceptance hours and permit conditions.

Required quantities, accepted-delivery/POD records and actual departure times are missing. Full OTIF is therefore not calculable from this file. In 3,068 of 7,729 consecutive-stop transitions, recorded arrival precedes the minimum implied by previous arrival, unloading and the next recorded drive. These inconsistencies prevent a reliable causal route replay without data-owner clarification.

The department states OTIF 97.2% and a 98.5% company target. These are case references, separate from the calculated arrival percentages (Department file: Outbound transport, 2026, pp. 2–3).

2 Research and evidence maturity

Recent academic article

Gerrits, van Heeswijk, and Mes (2024) systematically review 169 transport-logistics studies selected from 553 search results. They organise self-organising logistics around delegated decisions and useful cooperation. Hybrid control can combine local choices with human oversight. Their review distinguishes agent-based decomposition of a central problem from genuine delegation and cooperation.

The evidence has limited operational maturity: 115 reviewed studies are classified at simulation maturity and only five as deployed. The search ended in May 2023. The review covers transport broadly and does not test grocery unloading, FreshWay’s shared platform or its financial return. It supports a design framework and a testable hypothesis, rather than a benefit forecast (Gerrits et al., 2024, §§3–7).

Application to Outbound: our design inference

The shared server provides infrastructure. Arrival and unloading forecasts provide information. Outbound adds delegated choices only within a provisional planning workspace: trip agents choose candidate options, exchange feasibility and revise competing requests. A human still releases the operational plan. This is a proposed step toward partial self-organisation in planning; its implementation must demonstrate actual cooperation and adaptation.

The initial shadow test compares a central planner workflow against this bounded coordination mechanism using the same constraints. If the implementation only publishes forecasts or alerts and all planning decisions remain central, it must be described as predictive decision support.

4 Coordination and adaptation

A bounded multi-agent planning mechanism — proposal

The design delegates tentative option selection to per-trip agents. A reservation service protects mutually exclusive candidate slots against double claims; it applies a policy fixed by Outbound. It does not centrally optimise every route. Trip agents retain the local choice to request, withdraw and revise their options in response to other agents and new feasibility information.

StepInformation, delegated action and feedback
1. ObserveRead current approved TMS version, confirmed constraints, load response and valid predictions. Recompute risk when an input changes.
2. Choose locallyEach trip agent selects a feasible tentative option from its approved option set. Start with conditional 5-minute planning increments for un-departed trips. No real departure moves.
3. CoordinateAgents exchange candidate requests and window slack. The reservation service accepts a compatible provisional claim under the declared priority/tie rule or returns a conflict.
4. AdaptA conflicting trip agent releases or revises its request. A changed readiness response invalidates affected claims, which agents re-evaluate. Stop after a bounded negotiation limit and escalate unresolved conflict.
5. Review and releaseShow the mutually consistent candidate set, uncertainty and affected stops to the planner. Only an approved plan version can reach the TMS/driver.
6. EvaluateLog proposals, conflicts, adaptation, human decisions and outcomes. Model or policy changes require a separate human release.

Table 4. Design inference informed by Gerrits et al. (2024) and Lesson 6 (2026), slides 10–11 and 20–25. Option bounds, priority, tie-breaking, expiry and negotiation limits must be approved and tested before use.

Illustrative test, not an observed FreshWay event

Two trip agents request the same single earlier candidate slot. One receives a provisional reservation under the approved priority rule. The other revises its option instead of retaining a conflicting plan. If the first load becomes unavailable, that reservation expires and both agents adapt again. The planner then decides whether to change either real departure. The test succeeds only if logs show these delegated choices and feedback.

A provisional slot is a planning construct, not an acquired warehouse dock or charging reservation. Orderpicking can initially return feasibility manually; automatic warehouse scheduling needs its own agreement. The shared platform enables this exchange, but does not grant Outbound authority over another department.

Predictions, alerts and shared hosting alone do not satisfy the proposed self-organisation mechanism. Shadow demonstration comes before any operational claim.

Limits, escalation and reclaim

Permit only options that use valid, fresh data and verified store, vehicle, driver, load and energy constraints. Unknown restrictions block the affected option. Missing keys, plan-version conflicts, low-confidence inputs, stale data or unresolved negotiation cause escalation. No change can quietly move another department’s work or a contractual window.

The planner can disable coordination, clear tentative reservations and continue from the last approved TMS plan with a manual review queue. Log who disabled it, invalidated candidates and the reason. Test this recovery before live use. IT can isolate the service without taking over operational decision authority.

Cross-delivery — prepared for Orderpicking

Outbound offers Orderpicking a versioned departure-feasibility request and the proposed system-decision boundaries. The request identifies delivery/trip, load reference, current approved departure, candidate time, reason, source timestamp, plan version and expiry. Orderpicking uses it to return confirmed load availability and whether the candidate is feasible. It retains control over picking priorities.

The platform uses that response to withdraw or revise conflicting provisional options before the planner releases any actual change. Agree keys, response time, correction handling, owner and escalation in the Interface Agreement. This request/response contract is proposed, not an existing live feed.

Comparison and attribution

Compare current approved planning, simple statistical estimates with identical approval rules, and AI predictions with the proposed bounded coordination. Keep prediction horizons, constraints and data availability comparable. This separates the value of earlier scheduling, integration, predictions and coordination.

Split training and evaluation chronologically, keeping whole route-days together. Fit medians/preprocessing on training data only. Evaluate ZE/non-ZE, site, vehicle type and peak periods separately. Do not use future actual arrivals, the same stop’s actual unloading, final driving duration, costs or energy as pre-dispatch features.

For live evaluation, use matched or randomised route-days where feasible. Report counts, percentage-point change and uncertainty that respects route clustering. Agree a useful service improvement and acceptable cost, early-arrival, safety and other-store limits before results. A proposed four-week pilot may need extension for enough evidence. No binding 95% ZE norm, MAE 5-minute requirement or 2% early-arrival limit is established by the case.

Do not infer a safe MW charging budget by subtracting MW loads from an MVA contract without the appropriate electrical basis.

Costs, benefits and safeguards

Obtain incremental quotations for configuration, API access, integration, hosting, security, maintenance, training, monitoring and resilience. Do not count the existing EUR 0.45 million annual TMS contract as a new AI investment. Size hardware only after ordinary/peak benchmarks. Outbound owns its model and pilot work; IT operates shared infrastructure. Finance must agree a cost allocation method and avoid double-counting savings.

Potential value includes shared support and earlier useful planning decisions. Risks include common outages, resource contention, inaccurate forecasts, sensitive location data and additional review/warehouse/store work. Limit inference permissions, show data age and uncertainty, retain approved versions and test manual fallback. No equipment purchase, fleet sharing, charging automation, job reduction or net saving is assumed.
