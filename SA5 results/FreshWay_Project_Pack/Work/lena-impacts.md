## Concrete bounded decision handover and self-organisation

The design must give software a meaningful decision and feedback loop while keeping actual transport changes under the human approval agreed in SA4. Merely recalculating predictions, sending alerts or publishing one-way data does not prove self-organisation.

Recommended design proposition: **delegate provisional candidate-plan coordination across trip agents to the system, while the planner retains every actual operational change.** Each trip agent evaluates its own current stop risk and requests a provisional feasible departure option. Agents exchange window slack, candidate-slot use and feasibility status. A declared, deterministic coordination policy resolves conflicting provisional claims and re-evaluates affected trips when a risk or feasibility response changes. It produces one mutually consistent recommendation set. The Outbound planner then approves, edits or rejects execution in the TMS. The authority is limited to the planning workspace; a reservation is not a dispatch instruction, a changed picking priority, a store promise or a charging allocation.

Example test scenario (illustrative, not case observation): two not-yet-departed trips request a single feasible earlier candidate slot. Trip agents exchange updated risk and slack. When the higher-priority trip gains that provisional slot, the other agent recomputes its options and releases its conflicting claim. A new readiness response can reverse the provisional allocation. The planner sees a consistent plan and decides whether any real departure changes. The multi-agent system adapts its planning allocation; it never silently dispatches a truck.

| Handover item | Proposed content |
|---|---|
| Decision given to system | Allocate/release provisional candidate-plan reservations and rank a jointly feasible recommendation set among Outbound trip agents under an approved coordination policy. |
| Human decision retained | All actual departure shifts, route/stop changes, driver contact/instructions, external promises, subcontractor booking, picking priorities, charging allocations, model/boundary release and spending. |
| Preconditions | Validated fresh inputs; known store and vehicle/access constraints; one plan version; receiver-agreed readiness/quantity interface; documented policy with thresholds accepted before pilot. |
| Stop/escalate | Missing/stale data, inconsistent keys/plan versions, infeasible mutual allocation, unresolved C03/C10 constraints, low confidence or any action affecting another department beyond its agreement. |
| How to reclaim | Planner disables automated coordination, clears provisional reservations and continues in existing TMS. Log the last approved plan and each automated action; retain a manual queue and test recovery before live operation. |
| Acceptance evidence | Decision-rights policy signed/approved by the responsible Outbound human. Orderpicking separately writes whether the proposed request/response payload is usable. No confirmation is supplied by this AI review. |

Call this a **proposal for partial self-organisation in planning**. The pilot must demonstrate actual cooperative allocation/adaptation before claiming that the design meets the research definition. If the implementation only alerts a central planner and never allows agents to coordinate delegated choices, describe it as predictive decision support.

## Conflicts and cost allocation to retain in evaluation

- Earlier candidate departures can consume Orderpicking flexibility, receiving staff time and driver availability. The pilot must assess other stores and departments, not only the target ZE stops.
- Highest-risk-trip priority can repeatedly disadvantage low-risk/non-ZE trips. Check fairness and non-pilot service, and set priority rules before testing.
- Sharing platform hosting may reduce duplicated work but also adds access control, integration, support, migration and resilience costs. Quantify neither net savings nor server specifications until requirements and alternatives are compared.
- Shared resource ownership belongs to the responsible department. Outbound requests a constraint/availability answer; it does not acquire charging or consumer-delivery authority through a shared AI server.
- Store-specific forecasts require correct meaning and timing of inputs. Do not train on information unavailable when the prediction is issued. The data agent handles calculations and source-grain checks.
- C10 materially changes feasibility: 20 of 22 ZE stores have no trailer-compatible dock, and 37 residential stores carry pre-07:00 noise conditions. The card does not identify all 37 individually, so a per-store permit register remains a dependency.
- C03 materially changes subcontracting feasibility: diesel-only refrigerated store fleet, booking cutoff and unresolved ZE fine liability. A small time shift cannot fix vehicle access.
- C09 shows why average-day gains cannot justify procurement: test peak operation, price integration first, confirm power/space/cooling with Storage, and cost continuity/fallback.
