## Source evidence tiers

`Lessons/2026_Lesson 2 - Smart logistics and business intelligence.pptx`, slides 49-50, defines four tiers (the user's post mentions 1/2/3, but the course framework also has 4):

| Tier | Course meaning | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Running in production, quantified in accountable reporting; scale named; independent journalism has examined it | Investor or audited reporting carries management accountability; still distinguish company figures from independently measured outcomes |
| 2 | Deployment announced by the company with date, named technology partner/site count and independent trade press | Deployment evidence, not necessarily audited outcome proof |
| 3 | Pilot, proof of concept or plan | Real but unproven at operating scale; label clearly |
| 4 | Vendor blog, marketing or a figure with no traceable origin | Illustration only; never make it a business-case result |

The course test is customer/organisation, date and second independent source. A recent peer-reviewed academic study may provide strong conceptual/method evidence while still being simulation or a pilot. Peer review by itself does not satisfy production Tier 1. Score the particular claim and evidence type, not merely a whole source's prestige. The proposed FreshWay platform and models remain proposals, with prospective benefits labelled hypotheses.

The lesson 4 page reinforces checking who uses a technology, since when, at what scale and whether it is still running. Targets and forecasts cannot be copied forward as achieved results. External legal dates in teaching slides need current primary-source verification if repeated as legal claims.
