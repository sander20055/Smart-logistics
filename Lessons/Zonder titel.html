Lesson 1 — Introduction and company start-up

In this course you do not work in a project group. You work in a department of FreshWay, a Dutch supermarket chain that wants to make its logistics smarter. Lesson 1 is the day we set the company up: you get your department, your role and the people you will need. Nothing is solved yet today.

Before you come

  • Read this page. It takes about fifteen minutes and it is the only preparation for lesson 1.
  • Bring a laptop, and make sure you can log in to ChatGPT (Work) with your school account. Bring a laptop to every lesson from now on.
  • You do not have to read the assignment or the datasets yet. They are there for when you need them.

What we do in this lesson

Eight blocks, in this order. Two of them are blocks in which you work at your own table.

  1. Why this course exists, and who FreshWay is
  2. How this course works: you are a department, not a project group
  3. Forming the departments and dividing the five roles (at your table)
  4. Break
  5. Working well and critically with GenAI
  6. Setting up your department's agent team (at your table)
  7. The councils meet for the first time
  8. Close: five sentences that together become the FreshWay Working Agreement

How this course works

You are a department of FreshWay

FreshWay runs as a company for nine lessons. There are seven departments: Incoming transport, Storage, Orderpicking, Outbound transport, Inventory and supply chain management, E-commerce, and Delivery to consumers. Your lecturers are the Executive Board of that company — they take office in lesson 2. The departments are made beforehand and at random: in a company you do not pick your colleagues.

Two things happen every week. Your department needs something it cannot make itself, and your department delivers something another department is waiting for. That second one is called a cross-delivery: a short paragraph inside the work you hand in that week, saying what you deliver, to which department, and what they will use it for. The receiving department confirms in one line that they got it and that it was usable.

Five roles, one for each of you

You divide these inside your department, in the lesson. Each role has its own product that comes back every week — that is what you personally make, all semester.

So you have two seats: your department, and one council. A council has one person from every department, and it is the only place where the whole company meets across departments. That is why you never have to find out who to talk to — the meeting is already in the timetable. Inventory and supply chain management works with four people: there, Technology Officer and Business Case Officer are one person, who sits in the Investment Council.

Every lesson has the same rhythm

Nine lessons, one rhythm. You learn it once.

  1. Board opening and commitments — the Board walks through its decisions, and each manager answers for what the department promised last week
  2. Masterclass — the new tool of the week; this is the only new theory you get
  3. The five bodies meet — the Management Team and the four councils, all at the same time; this is where you get what your department lacks
  4. Department work — the only moment all five of you sit together, with what everyone brought back
  5. Board close — your manager says out loud what your department will have ready by the next lesson

Two rules that never change

  • The bodies always meet before department work. First you collect what you do not have, then you build.
  • Every lesson ends with a commitment, and the next lesson opens with the check on it. The work itself happens between the lessons and is handed in on Brightspace before the next one.

Why it is built this way

Group work usually fails on goodwill: everyone writes their own chapter and someone staples it together at the end. Here the dependency is built into the structure instead. You need another department's data to answer your own question, and they need yours. Two instruments hold that together, and you need neither of them today:

  • the Board Decision Log — the Board's numbered, dated record of every decision. A decision that is not in the log does not exist, and you may appeal to the log and to nothing else. It opens in lesson 2.
  • the Interface Agreement — the deal between two departments about which data moves from one to the other. It is signed by the department that receives the data, because they decide whether it is usable. The first ones are signed in lesson 3.

Two more instruments follow in lesson 2, when the Board takes office.

What counts

  • Eight sub-assignments, one per lesson, which together become one portfolio. You agree at the end of each lesson what is ready by the next one.
  • One group grade, adjusted per person by peer assessment.
  • A final assessment: fifteen minutes presenting, twenty minutes of questions. Every member must be able to explain the whole solution — including the council you sat in, not only your own chapter.
  • Attendance: at least eight of the nine lessons. If you are not there, a council seat stays empty, so your department sends someone in your place.
  • The full assignment, study guide and rubric are on Brightspace. Read the rubric before lesson 2.

Read this before the lesson

Smart logistics, in short

Logistics is no longer about moving boxes. Three pressures changed it at the same time: globalisation (chains are longer and more fragile), sustainability (emissions, energy and the electricity grid are now design constraints, not afterthoughts), and digitalisation (every movement leaves data). Customers expect faster, cheaper and greener at once, and decisions increasingly run on data rather than on experience alone.

The step after that is the one this course is really about: more and more, systems decide instead of people — which order goes on which van, when a replenishment is triggered. If a system decides, someone has to choose which decision to hand over, and on what evidence. That someone is a technical business engineer.

And yet the sector is not there. Of all Dutch companies, about one in six used AI in 2025; the EU average is around 20%; in transport and storage it is 6 to 7% (CBS, 2025). Both stories are true at once: the front-runners are far ahead, and the sector as a whole is not. That gap is where your advice lands.

FreshWay, the company you work for

FreshWay is a full-service supermarket formula, not a discounter. It has 120 stores, three distribution centres (one central, two regional), one e-fulfilment centre and five delivery hubs, and about 311,000 cases — boxes or crates — going to the stores every day. Revenue is 2.43 billion euro a year, of which roughly 10% is online. There are 450 suppliers, and about a third of them still send no electronic despatch note.

Two numbers matter more than the rest: FreshWay has 17 million euro a year to invest in logistics, and that is not enough for seven departments' proposals. That is where this course ends up. The full picture is in the Company dossier, in Company info > About FreshWay, which opens today.

Working well and critically with GenAI

In this course you may use GenAI, and we encourage it. You stay responsible: a product that is entirely generated is not your product, and how you used it is part of what we assess. The question is not whether you use it, but how.

Table 2. The difference that decides whether it helps you
Offloading Scaffolding

You hand over the thinking.

You hand over the work, not the judgement.

The output is the answer.

The output is a draft you check.

You cannot explain it without the tool.

You can explain it without the tool.

The rule of thumb: if you cannot explain it without the tool, it is not yours yet. At the final assessment every member has to explain everything, so this is not a moral point — it is a practical one.

The second half is verification. GenAI produces fluent text, and fluency is not evidence: a generated source can look perfect and still not exist. Ask three questions of every claim you take over.

  • Who? Who is the customer, the author or the organisation behind this claim?
  • When? What is the date, and is it still true?
  • Who else? Is there a second, independent source that says the same?

This is not an extra: a critical selection of multiple credible sources is the first criterion of the rubric. Finally, every portfolio contains an AI declaration: which tool, and what for. The wording is in the assignment, and the nine agency labels you can refer to are on Brightspace. Copying generated text literally is fraud and fails the course.

Your department's agent team

An agent team is a small set of assistants you configure yourself in ChatGPT (Work): a coordinator that splits up the question and delegates, and a researcher that does the looking up — both working from a project folder with the FreshWay sources in it. It mirrors what you do in the classroom: someone keeps the direction, someone else does the work.

We set up one team per department, not one per student. The Data & Interface Officer owns the project folder. In the lesson we do the setting up together and ask the team one test question — that is all. Actually using it comes afterwards, as department work between lesson 1 and lesson 2. One working laptop per department is enough on the day.

Tip

Read the five roles in Table 1 before you come. Dividing them takes ten minutes in the lesson, and it goes a lot better if everyone already knows what they are choosing between. If you are not agreed within those ten minutes, the Board assigns the roles.

Where to find what

Brightspace Teams

The course information, the company information and the hand-in are here. Everything about FreshWay sits in one folder, Company info, laid out the way the company is:

  • About FreshWay — for everyone, from today: the Company dossier, the FreshWay glossary — twenty words and the guide to the datasets. The data that belongs to the company as a whole is added at the end of lesson 2.
  • Forms and templates — the documents you fill in: charters, memos, agreements, meeting minutes, council and debrief cards. You will come back here almost every week.
  • 01 Incoming transport up to 07 Delivery to consumers — one folder per department, holding the information and the data of that department together. You see your own, and no one else's. The information is there from today; the data follows at the end of lesson 2.
  • Some material is sensitive or strategic and is not published at all. It exists, and you ask the Board for it. How you do that, and the index of what exists, come in lesson 2.

All the data appears at the end of lesson 2, in the same folder as the information it belongs to. You need it in lesson 3, and by then you also need a question worth asking it — that is what lessons 1 and 2 are for.

Handing in always happens on Brightspace, never in Teams. That way there is one administration and one version.

Teams is your workplace: one team, FreshWay — Smart Logistics 2026. You are added to your channels in lesson 1, once the departments and the roles are known.

  • Three open channels, for what belongs to the whole company: 01 Board (the Board Decision Log, the minutes of the Management Team, the Board Memos), 02 Registers (the Technology Register and the Investment Register), and 03 Cross-deliveries (one folder per lesson).
  • Twelve private channels: A1 to A7, one per department, and B1 to B5, one per council. You are in two of them, because you have two seats. Department channels are private on purpose — if you could read along in another department's folder, the Interface Agreement would be pointless.

Two habits keep it usable. One place per thing: what concerns the whole company goes in an open channel, what is yours goes in your own channel, and something that only exists in a chat does not exist. And do not copy the company dossier or the datasets into Teams — link to Brightspace instead, or within three weeks you will have two versions that disagree.

What you leave with, and what comes next

  • Your Department Charter: who you are, who does which role, what your department wants to achieve, and two things you already know you need from another department. You hand it in during the lesson; it does not count towards your 10,000 words.
  • The five sentences your councils write, which together are the draft FreshWay Working Agreement. It goes on Brightspace the same day, and you sign it next lesson.
  • Before lesson 2: read the Company dossier and your own department's file, and finish setting up your agent team with your department (chapter 4 of the manual).
  • In lesson 2: the Executive Board takes office and the Working Agreement is signed, each department shows in sixty seconds that its agent team works, and the masterclass is about smart logistics and business intelligence.
